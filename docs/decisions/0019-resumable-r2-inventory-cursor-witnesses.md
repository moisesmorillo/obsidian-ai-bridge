# ADR 0019: Resumable cursor-witness journal for isolated R2 inventories

## Status

**Accepted for isolated M7.4 local TDD after independent corrective design review** investigate-5eb505b68c8fa1e53b3ca98d11123244 (ready for local TDD with bounded gates). Not implemented or qualified. This acceptance is not authorization for sync activation, remote Workers Free qualification, deployment, push or a completion claim. [Corrective design and state table](../plans/2026-10-02-m7-inventory-cursor-history-design.md) and the M7 specification govern implementation. Canonical when merged.

## Context

M7 rejects a repeated R2 LIST cursor but the current v1 manifest/replay checks only adjacent equality. R2 cursors are opaque and Cloudflare documents no all-pages uniqueness. An empty truncated sequence `A → B → A → terminal` can falsely complete a scan. A full 20,000-digest history cannot fit the current 8,192-byte manifest. Reading all earlier chunks before each advance would require up to 199,990,000 extra reads. A first design using one permanent UUID claim was independently rejected: crash after claim before target dispatch permanently stranded the same-ID scan. Two read-only design reviews found authority and resource gates open (investigate-e50c0a5d3965ba51bac7e89c2dd595a2 and investigate-4d749dfeb6b732dee7f78964f1afab19).

## Decision

- Introduce strict manifest schemaVersion **2** for newly admitted scans; preserve protocolMajor 1 and historic v1 decoding for no-reuse tombstones/expired cleanup only. A v1 scan, even labeled complete, cannot resume, return a handle or page, or be silently upgraded. Add required `cursorWitnessMode: 1`. Raise **only the v2** manifest family body cap to **9,216 bytes** with maximal-field byte fixtures; v1 remains at 8,192. The accepted ADR 0018 no-reuse tombstone rule remains, at up to 9,216 bytes per new v2 ID plus 8,192 per historic v1 ID and key/metadata overhead. Without account admission/quota this retention is not proven operationally sustainable.
- For each truncated output cursor create an immutable, deterministic SHA-256-digest-keyed witness that binds its producing step, vault/scan ID and verified chunk hash; cap its strict canonical record at 384 UTF-8 bytes. Before the first PUT create an exact-CAS per-step attempt journal bound to the same chunk/digest, with closed `attempting` (fresh UUID generation) and `retry_wait` (persisted conservative floor) states; cap it at 512 bytes. A digest-index collision or previous-step witness fails the scan; an unbounded raw cursor never enters a key or journal. At most 20,000 journals and 20,000 witnesses can exist for one scan, with ≤17,920,000 record-body bytes plus keys/metadata, separately from ≤192 MiB chunk evidence.
- The journal is **retryable**. A winning own-UUID generation permits at most one create-only witness PUT in its invocation; a crash before dispatch or an unknown target response is reconciled by exact witness read. With absent witness, exact-CAS to `retry_wait` after the journal key's own cooldown, recording at least observation+1,100 ms, every floor already persisted in the observed journal and each response floor available to that transition. A fresh `attempting` generation requires both stored floor and journal cooldown. The caller must retain and honor a returned floor before its own next attempt; an uncertain floor CAS is not dispatch authority. Do not claim a lifetime count of physical PUT attempts. Concurrent old/new PUTs write identical bound bytes with atomic create-only predicates; their responses are not progress authority. A stale generation cannot authorize a new dispatch. Exact current journal/manifest/slot/expiry observation precedes each dispatch but is not a lease.
- A target response that first arrives **after** another generation has dispatched cannot retroactively prohibit that earlier dispatch. The guaranteed floor is the maximum available to the dispatching invocation from its response/caller context and durable journal observation; a separate isolate cannot know an unpersisted late result. A response floor learned before a future dispatch by its owner must be honored and carried to the next eligible `retry_wait` CAS; until that floor is durable, that owner cannot dispatch again. After a crash, only persisted floors and conservative observed-time cooldowns can be reconstructed. These are availability/backoff constraints, **not** the no-overwrite or no-false-complete proof: atomic create-only and exact evidence remain mandatory even when overlapping PUTs occur. This clarification was confirmed by the owner after focused review investigate-3c1ef7fbb034aa3a29f426c4df9af9c2; it applies only to this isolated cursor-witness journal, not mutation attempt-state floors.
- Every fresh/replayed truncated chunk must be strictly verified together with its same-step journal and exact digest witness **before** original-generation manifest advancement. A terminal chunk has no new cursor witness, but final vector/complete CAS/slot release/handle and pages are reachable only via these fenced transitions. Null initial input cursor is not visited; every truncated output is nonempty and new. Read-back/conditional-null/unknown or absent evidence never implies completion; expiry/late PUT can create only orphan scratch under a permanently retained manifest.
- Extend **only expired-scan scoped cleanup** to allow canonical step-journal and cursor-witness keys as well as numeric chunks, one unconditional R2 DELETE plus absence read-back per invocation. Never delete a manifest. N≤60,001 scratch objects and ~5N+3≤300,008 no-retry cleanup calls are illustrative envelopes, **not** retry-inclusive lifetime bounds or guaranteed same-day reclamation. Empty prefix observation is not permanent absence in the face of late PUT.

## Consequences and pending proof

The first-success illustrative scan calls are ~500,014 across invocations before unknown/retry work, and a candidate 32-call page-step preflight, 136-call start, 134-call finalization, 18-call evidence page and 8-call cleanup reserve are analytically below the existing 400-call **physical** per-invocation cap. All paths still need counted adapter traces, byte-cap tests, lost-response/late-PUT races, and a real in-isolate workerd composed-store test; synthetic CPU allowances do **not** prove Workers Free's 10-ms CPU ceiling. Workers Free's 100,000 incoming requests/day, 1,000 internal-service calls/request and R2 Free's 1 million Class A operations/month and 10 GB-month storage need an admission/qualification policy before activation. No account-wide frequency or permanent-manifest quota is established here. A scan can stay uncertain until expiry under infrastructure failure; it may not return a false complete handle.

## Alternatives

- Fixed rolling window or probabilistic filter: misses long cycles or falsely rejects valid scans.
- Compare against all prior chunks: too many extra R2 reads at 20,001 pages.
- One-shot UUID claim and no retry: rejected after an independent crash-before-dispatch counterexample violated same-ID replay.
- Put all cursor digests in the manifest: exceeds its bounded record limit.
